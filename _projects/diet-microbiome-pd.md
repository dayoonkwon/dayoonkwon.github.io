The gut is a leading candidate for where Parkinson's disease (PD) begins, and diet is the most direct lever on the gut microbiome. I study how diet relates to microbial composition and function in people living with PD.

Among PD patients in central California, higher diet quality and fiber intake were associated with more putative anti-inflammatory, butyrate-producing bacteria, whereas higher added sugar intake was associated with more putative pro-inflammatory bacteria. Predicted metagenomic profiles pointed the same way, with fewer bacterial genes for lipopolysaccharide biosynthesis among patients with healthier diets {% cite kwon2024_diet_microbiome_pd %}.

Integrating the gut microbiome with the serum metabolome in the same patients showed that PD-associated gut bacteria relate to host lipid metabolism and immune-related pathways {% cite zhang2026_metab_microbiome_pd %}.

Together, these findings suggest that a healthy diet may support a gut environment with less inflammation in PD. The longer-term aim is to connect diet, microbial function, and host metabolism to PD progression.
